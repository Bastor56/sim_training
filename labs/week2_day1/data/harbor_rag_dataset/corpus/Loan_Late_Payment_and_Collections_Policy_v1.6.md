# Loan Late Payment and Collections Policy

_Policy number: LN-COL-002 | Version 1.6 | Effective date: May 1, 2026 | Owner: Consumer Lending and Collections_

## 1. Purpose

This policy	describes how Harbor Credit Union treats consumer loan payments that are not received by the due date, the fees	that apply, the communications members receive, and the point at which an account is referred for collection. It is	intended to balance fair treatment of members experiencing difficulty with the credit union's duty to protect the membership as	a whole from loan losses.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to closed-end consumer loans, including new and used auto loans, personal loans,	and share-secured loans. Credit card accounts, home equity lines of credit and first mortgages are covered by their own	agreements and servicing procedures.

## 3. Grace Period

A payment is considered late if it is not received within 15 calendar days after the	due date shown on the billing statement. Payments received during the grace period are applied as on-time payments for	fee purposes, although interest continues to accrue daily on simple-interest loans until the payment is received.

If the last day	of the grace period falls on a weekend or a Federal Reserve holiday, the grace period is extended to	the next business day.

## 4. Late Fees

When a payment is late under Section 3, Harbor assesses a single late fee equal to	5% of the scheduled payment amount or $25.00, whichever is less. Only one late fee is charged for any	one scheduled payment, and a late fee is never charged because an earlier late fee remains unpaid. Late fees	are not charged on loans enrolled in an approved hardship program while the member is meeting the program terms.

## 5. Delinquency Communications

1.	Day 5 after the due date: courtesy reminder by e-mail or text alert.
2. Day 16: late notice by mail	stating the late fee and the amount now due.
3. Day 30: outbound call from a Collections Specialist to discuss	the reason for the delay and repayment options.
4. Day 45: second written notice offering a hardship review appointment.
5. Day	60: notice of right to cure, where required by state law.

## 6. Credit Reporting

Harbor reports an account as delinquent to the consumer	reporting agencies once a payment is 30 or more days past due. Reporting is accurate and complete, and disputes	of credit reporting are investigated within the timeframes required by the Fair Credit Reporting Act.

## 7. Collections Referral

Accounts that reach 90 days	past due are transferred from the Collections Specialist team to the Recovery Unit. The Recovery Unit may recommend repossession	of collateral, legal action, or placement with an outside agency, subject to approval by the Collections Manager. All collection	activity must comply with applicable consumer protection laws, and members may not be contacted before 8:00 a.m. or after	9:00 p.m. local time.

## 8. Hardship Assistance

Members experiencing temporary hardship may request a payment extension, a skip-a-payment, or a loan modification. A	loan may receive no more than two payment extensions in any rolling 12-month period. Hardship requests are reviewed within	five business days of receiving the member's completed application and supporting documents.

## 9. Charge-Off

Consumer loans that remain delinquent are charged off	no later than 120 days past due for closed-end loans, unless the Collections Manager documents a reasonable expectation of	recovery. Charge-off does not release the member from the obligation to repay.
